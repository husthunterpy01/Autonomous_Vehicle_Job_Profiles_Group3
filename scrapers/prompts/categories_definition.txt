UNIFIED JOB-PROFILE CATEGORY TAXONOMY
These jobs have already been confirmed AV-relevant. Do not re-decide relevance: every job gets a category from this list, including one whose real work is platform/compute rather than the driving stack (see Infrastructure below) - never a reason to withhold a category or treat the job as out of scope.
The 10 categories below group into 5 technical areas:
- Perception & Sensing area: Sensing, Perception
- Localization & Mapping area: Localization, Mapping
- Decision area: Planning, Prediction
- System area: Control, Vehicle Interface, System and Safety
- Platform area: Infrastructure
Pick the ONE technical area that best matches the posting's primary responsibilities, then assign one or more categories from that area only - a posting may span multiple categories when responsibilities overlap, but only within that single area. Do not mix categories from different areas on the same posting. Weight multi-word, platform-specific phrases more heavily than broad single words.
DECIDE FROM THE TITLE FIRST. Read the job title before anything else: if it names one of the 10 categories or an unambiguous synonym of one (e.g. "Motion Planning Engineer", "Perception & Fusion Engineer", "Localization Engineer", "GNC Engineer" for Control, "Sensor Validation Engineer" for Sensing, "System Safety Engineer"/"Safety Evaluation"/"Safety Methodologies"/"Platform Safety Assurance" for System and Safety), that IS the category - assign it and stop, regardless of what the company's boilerplate "about us" paragraph says elsewhere in the posting. A title-named category is only overridden by the body text when the body describes clearly different day-to-day responsibilities than the title implies (e.g. a "Perception Engineer" posting whose actual duties are entirely about the cloud training pipeline, not detection/tracking work) - overriding on boilerplate mission language is never valid grounds. Only when the title itself is generic (e.g. "Software Engineer", "Staff Engineer II") or names no category do you move to the body text: read the responsibilities/requirements section for what the role does day to day, using each category's Context below - not just its Keywords - to judge the real fit. A posting can genuinely span two categories: "LiDAR sensor calibration and point-cloud object detection" is both Sensing (the calibration work) and Perception (the object detection work).
WORD-SENSE TRAPS: some words appear in a job posting with an everyday/business meaning that has nothing to do with the category whose name or keyword they happen to match - check which sense is actually meant before deciding on the word alone.
- "Platform" is not, by itself, a signal for Infrastructure. "Platform Safety Assurance", "Vehicle Platform Engineer", "AV Platform Integration" all use "platform" to mean the vehicle/product platform (the autonomy stack, the robot, the car), a completely different sense from Infrastructure's main_type name "Platform" (cloud/compute/MLOps).
- "Localizing"/"localization" in a business sense (localizing a product, team, or hiring for a specific country/market - "localising our AV technology for the Japanese market") is market/regional adaptation, not the Localization category (determining the vehicle's own pose). Only pose/positioning work (SLAM, NDT, GPS/IMU fusion) is the category.
- "Control" in "Body Control(s)" or "Body Control Module/ECU" refers to a vehicle's non-driving electrical subsystems (doors, lights, windows, HVAC), not the Control category (executing a planned trajectory via steering/throttle/brake). A "Body Control Systems Engineer" belongs under Vehicle Interface (embedded integration with vehicle hardware/actuators), not Control.
- "Pipeline", "platform", "framework", "tooling", "continuous evaluation pipeline", "CI/CD", "release gating", "automated workflows", and "simulation platform/environment" do not by themselves signal Infrastructure, no matter how many of them show up together. What matters is the PURPOSE the pipeline/platform/tooling serves, not that "platform" or "tooling" language is present. If its purpose is evaluating or validating the AV STACK itself - test scenario libraries, evaluation metrics/KPIs for readiness/safety decisions, risk assessment, release gating criteria, statistical validation metrics supporting a Safety Case, robustness/regression testing frameworks, vehicle dynamics models built for closed-loop V&V or RL training - that is System and Safety (safety/quality validation), even when the posting is dense with "platform", "pipeline", "framework", and "simulation" language describing the tooling used to do it. Infrastructure is for tooling/pipelines whose purpose is the engineering team's OWN compute/build/deploy process (CI/CD for software builds, ML training infrastructure, data infrastructure, cloud/compute platforms) - never for a validation/simulation platform whose purpose is testing or certifying the vehicle's autonomy stack. A "Simulation Vehicle Modeling" or "Autonomy Evaluation"-titled role that owns validation metrics, safety-case statistics, or V&V test frameworks for the autonomy stack is System and Safety, regardless of how much platform/pipeline/framework language describes the tooling it builds to do that.
- "Infrastructure" as a plain English word for physical test-lab equipment and setup ("test infrastructure", "test rig", thermal chambers, vibe tables, dynos, environmental chambers) is not the Infrastructure category. A hardware validation/test engineer who owns environmental and vibration testing, RCCA (root cause and corrective action), and design/production validation of the vehicle's hardware kit is doing hands-on hardware validation work - System and Safety (or Sensing, if the hardware under test is specifically the sensors) - even when the posting calls them a "test infrastructure owner". The Infrastructure category is earned only by cloud/compute/MLOps/CI-CD/data-pipeline/chip-design work, never by "infrastructure" describing a physical test bench.
Decide every category, including Infrastructure, on the actual responsibilities described - never on a word matching a category or main_type's own name/label.
A company's self-description of its own business - "we build the infrastructure/platform/tools for X", "we're powering the future of physical AI" - describes the company, not this specific role, and must never be read as evidence for Infrastructure (or any category) on its own. This is the same failure mode as generic AI/ML language below, and it is worth naming separately because several real companies' mission statements happen to contain the literal word "infrastructure" - a "Perception Engineer" at such a company is still Perception, not Infrastructure, no matter how the company describes itself. Infrastructure is earned by the role's own responsibilities (cloud/compute/MLOps/CI/CD/data-pipeline/chip work), the same as every other category.
Generic AI/ML terminology - "machine learning", "deep learning", "neural network", "AI" - appears across nearly every category here (Prediction models are ML, Planning can be learned, even Control can use learned MPC) and in almost any modern AV job's boilerplate company description, regardless of the actual role. Perception is not "the ML category": never assign a category on the strength of generic AI/ML language alone. The same goes for a dataset or benchmark name (nuScenes, KITTI, Argoverse, Waymo Open Dataset, BDD100K) - useful supporting evidence that a posting is real AV/ML engineering work, never sufficient by itself to decide which of the 10 categories applies, since the same dataset shows up in Perception, Prediction, and general ML work alike. Only the category's actual responsibilities - its Context and Keywords below - should drive that decision.
Generic safety/reliability language - "ensuring the safety and reliability of our systems", "building safe and reliable autonomy", "safety is our top priority" - is a stock phrase describing the GOAL of virtually every AV engineering role (a Perception, Prediction, or general data science posting will say this just as often as an actual safety engineer's), not evidence for System and Safety on its own. System and Safety is earned by actual safety-case, functional-safety (ISO 26262, SOTIF), fault-handling/diagnostics, or safety-critical evaluation/validation responsibilities - the same standard as every other category's Keywords/Context, not by any role's boilerplate claim that its work contributes to overall vehicle safety. A "Behavior Evaluation" data scientist whose actual work is evaluating behavior-prediction model performance is Prediction, not System and Safety, even though the posting also says its models "ensure the safety and reliability of our systems".

Sensing — sensor integration, calibration, and raw data acquisition from vehicle sensors.
Keywords: camera_driver, lidar_driver, radar_driver, gnss_driver, imu_driver, LiDAR, camera, radar, point cloud, velodyne, distortion correction, ring_outlier_filter, sensor calibration, sensor integration, data acquisition
Context: Works with the physical sensor hardware and its raw data feed - writing or maintaining camera/LiDAR/radar drivers, calibrating sensors against each other and the vehicle frame, and getting clean, synchronized point clouds and images out to the rest of the stack. This is upstream of Perception: Sensing gets the raw data flowing and calibrated; Perception is what makes sense of it.

Localization — determining the vehicle's own pose within its environment.
Keywords: ndt_scan_matcher, ekf_localizer, gyro_odometer, NDT (Normal Distributions Transform), pose estimation, dead reckoning, twist estimator, SLAM, HD-map-relative localization
Context: Answers "where is the vehicle right now, precisely" - matching live sensor data against a known map (NDT, SLAM) or fusing GPS/IMU/odometry to estimate pose. Distinct from Mapping, which builds the map itself rather than locating the vehicle within it.

Perception — detecting and understanding objects and the scene around the vehicle.
Keywords: object_recognition, object detection, object tracking, multi_object_tracker, euclidean_cluster, roi_cluster_fusion, camera-lidar fusion, sensor fusion, semantic segmentation, panoptic segmentation, traffic light recognition, YOLO, YOLOX, TensorRT, computer vision, obstacle detection, 2D/3D object detection
Context: Turns sensor data into an understanding of the scene - detecting, classifying, and tracking dynamic and static objects (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists, traffic lights, lane markings) from camera/LiDAR/radar input, often with a learned model. The category-deciding signal is WHAT is being detected or understood, not that a model or "AI" is involved - Prediction and Planning also use learned models, but for forecasting behavior and choosing a path, not scene understanding itself.

Prediction — forecasting the future behavior or trajectory of other agents.
Keywords: trajectory prediction, motion forecasting, interaction prediction, behavior modeling, behavior prediction, behavior evaluation, Lane Sequence Predictor, Inter-TNT, Free Move Predictor
Context: Forecasts what OTHER agents (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists) will do next - their future trajectory or behavior - so the planner can react to it. Downstream of Perception, which says what is there right now; upstream of Planning, which decides what the ego vehicle should do about it.

Planning — deciding the vehicle's intended path or maneuver.
Keywords: mission_planner, behavior_path_planner, motion_velocity_planner, lane change, lane following, obstacle avoidance, path smoother, path optimizer, scenario selector, freespace planner, motion planning, trajectory generation, EM Planner
Context: Decides the ego vehicle's own intended path or maneuver - lane changes, merges, the route through an intersection - given the current scene and the predicted behavior of others. Distinct from Control, which executes that chosen path through actuation rather than deciding it.

Control — executing the planned trajectory through vehicle actuation.
Keywords: trajectory_follower, lateral controller, longitudinal controller, MPC, model predictive control, PID control, vehicle_cmd_gate, throttle, brake, steering
Context: Takes an already-decided trajectory and computes the actual steering/throttle/brake commands to follow it (PID, MPC). The last software step before Vehicle Interface hardware actuation: Control decides WHAT commands to send, Vehicle Interface delivers them to the hardware.

Vehicle Interface — embedded integration between software and vehicle hardware/actuators.
Keywords: vehicle_interface, raw_vehicle_cmd_converter, drive-by-wire, CAN bus, pacmod, CanBus, embedded vehicle integration
Context: Builds the embedded, low-level bridge between the software stack's commands and the vehicle's physical actuators - CAN bus messaging, drive-by-wire integration, hardware abstraction. Distinct from Control, which decides the commands rather than delivering them to hardware.

Mapping — building or consuming HD maps and geospatial map data.
Keywords: lanelet2, HD map, vector map, pointcloud map, map projection, mapping, map engineering, geospatial systems
Context: Builds or maintains the HD map itself - lane geometry, map layers, vector or point-cloud maps - that Localization and Planning consume. Distinct from Localization, which uses the map to determine where the vehicle is rather than building the map.

System and Safety — fault handling, diagnostics, functional safety, and system-level monitoring.
Keywords: diagnostics, emergency_handler, fail-safe, system monitor, safety monitoring, autoware_state, functional safety, HMI, safety case, safety assurance, safety engineer, safety evaluation, safety methodology, safety operator, system safety, operational safety, safety validation, ISO 26262, SOTIF, hazard analysis
Context: Cross-cutting reliability work spanning the whole stack - fault detection, diagnostics, functional-safety compliance, fail-safe behavior, system-level health monitoring, building and validating the safety case, and safety-critical evaluation/validation/operations roles - rather than any single perception, planning, or control function. A role titled or described around "safety" (system safety, safety case, safety evaluation, safety methodology, safety operator) belongs here even without the word "functional" attached - it is still safety engineering, not a generic corporate or workplace-safety role, as long as it concerns the vehicle/autonomy system rather than office/factory-floor safety.

Infrastructure — cloud, compute, and MLOps platform work supporting AV development, not the driving stack itself.
Keywords: cloud infrastructure, AWS, GCP, Azure, Kubernetes, Docker, CI/CD, MLOps, ML platform, model serving, distributed training, GPU cluster, data pipeline, data infrastructure, DevOps, build system, compute infrastructure, developer tooling, chip design, ASIC, FPGA, compiler, GPU driver
Context: Builds and operates the compute, data, and deployment platform AV engineering runs on - cloud/on-prem infrastructure, ML training or serving platforms, CI/CD and build systems, data pipelines, developer tooling, or the underlying chip/hardware compute stack (GPU/ASIC/FPGA design, drivers, compilers) - rather than any part of the vehicle's own sensing-to-actuation software stack. This is the category for a role that is genuinely AV-company engineering work (already confirmed AV-relevant) whose actual day-to-day responsibilities are platform, tooling, or hardware-compute work rather than one of the 9 driving-stack categories above: use it instead of forcing a weak fit into an unrelated stack category, and instead of treating the job as out of scope.

SKILL NORMALIZATION
Normalize extracted skill names while keeping them recognizable. Examples:
ROS2, ROS 2 -> ROS 2
lidar, LiDAR, LIDAR -> LiDAR
pointcloud, point cloud -> Point Cloud
MPC, model predictive control -> Model Predictive Control
NDT, Normal Distributions Transform -> NDT
CyberRT, Cyber RT, Apollo Cyber -> Cyber RT
Waymo dataset, Waymo Open Dataset -> Waymo Open Dataset
