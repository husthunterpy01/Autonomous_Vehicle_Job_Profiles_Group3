## 2. Task Tracker Walkthrough

Martin shared his screen and introduced the GitHub project used to track Sprint 1 tasks and future sprints. Key points covered:

- Tasks carry a priority, size/effort estimate (in days), start date, target date, and an assignee; tasks should be finished by the target date.
- Task status values: Not Started, In Progress, Blocked, Done – to be kept up to date by the Scrum Master, with notes on any blockers or absences.
- Tasks link out to the corresponding GitHub Issues.
- Effort is estimated in days needed to complete a task, evaluated per sprint and per member.
- The tracker also includes an absence tracker (members note their own reasons for being unavailable), a meeting tracker (client, facilitator, and internal meetings, with agenda/status/notes), member roles by sprint, and a summary of important links (repository, reports, Canvas/design docs).
- The Scrum Master role rotates each sprint; Sprint 1 roles were pre-assigned by Martin, and a new Scrum Master will be assigned for Sprint 2.

## 3. Discussion: Effort Estimation and Deadlines

Harshil raised a question about how "effort" is calculated, and whether tasks assigned close to the weekend unfairly reduce a member's effort score if personal commitments limit available time.

Martin clarified:

- Effort is an estimate of the number of days to complete a task (not calendar months), used purely for planning/estimation purposes.
- The expected deadline for a task is generally one week from assignment (e.g. a task assigned on a Thursday is expected by the following Thursday at the latest).
- Finishing before the following Thursday counts as full effort (3/3); finishing later is recorded as late, though this is not intended to penalise members unfairly – urgent cases are treated flexibly.

Harshil confirmed this made sense.
